Move-Out Cleaning Guide Before Handing Over Your Keys

Moving out is stressful enough without worrying about your deposit. The good news is that most deposit deductions come from avoidable cleaning misses — the greasy oven nobody inspected, the dusty cabinet top, the grout line the inspector always checks. This guide walks through exactly what a professional move-out clean covers, room by room.

1. Read Your Lease: Know the Exact Standard

Before you lift a cloth, re-read the cleaning clause in your lease. Many landlords require the property to be returned in the same condition as move-in day, and some even reference "professional standard". Check your move-in inventory report — if you noted marks or stains when you arrived, photograph them again now so you are never charged for pre-existing damage.

2. The Kitchen: Where Deposits Are Won or Lost

Kitchens cause more deductions than any other room. Inspectors look closely at the oven, hob, extractor and splashback — the three things people rush. Strip the oven racks and soak them, degrease the interior thoroughly and wipe behind the hob. Empty every single cabinet and drawer, wipe the interiors, and leave the fridge defrosted with the door propped open and every shelf clean.

A thorough final wipe of the splashback and worktops in one direction removes every streak. Then run the extractor fan on high for ten minutes with the filters soaked clean — greasy extractor filters are a classic deduction trigger.

3. Bathrooms: Grout, Glass and Hidden Mould

Bathrooms need the same forensic detail. Descale the showerhead, clean the glass shower screen so it is streak-free and bright, and work on grout lines with a stiff brush. Mould around seals and silicon — especially near the bath and around the window frame — is a near-certain deduction, so treat it early and allow drying time.

Do not forget the mirror, the back of the toilet and the base of the pedestal. Inspectors run a finger along the top of the cistern and the bottom edge of the cabinet — surfaces nobody ever cleans until it is too late.

4. Bedrooms and Living Areas: Walls, Floors and Closets

Work top to bottom in every room. Dust ceiling corners and light fittings, wipe skirting boards and doors (including the tops), then clean the walls. For scuff marks on paint, a damp magic sponge works wonders on most finishes — test in an inconspicuous spot first. Closets are frequently inspected, so vacuum the floors and shelf liners, and leave doors ajar to air them out.

An inspector will always look where you do not think they can see — behind the door, above the wardrobe, under the sink. Beat them to every one of those spots.

If the property has carpet, arrange a professional steam clean before handover. A home machine rarely meets the extraction standard inspectors expect, and the difference shows in the inspection report.

5. Windows, Balconies and the Final Sweep

Clean every window inside and out where accessible — sills, tracks and frames included. If you have a balcony, sweep and mop it and clear any debris from the drainage point. Once every room is finished, do a final floor sweep from the back of the property to the front, then a last check of switches, handles and the inside of the letterbox. Remove every single item you left behind — a single forgotten bag of clothes is enough for an inspector to note "clean up required".

6. The Handover Walkthrough: Photograph Everything

When the property is spotless, document it. Walk through every room and film a slow video while taking dated photos of each area — including the inside of the oven, fridge, cabinets and bathrooms. These timestamps are your proof if a deposit dispute arises weeks later. Run the final handover checklist before you leave:

  • Every room photographed and filmed with a dated timestamp.
  • All personal belongings removed — including cupboards, closets and the loft.
  • Keys, fobs, garage remotes and access cards all collected for handover.
  • Signed handover sheet obtained and a copy kept for your records.
  • Final reading taken of the meter and utility accounts notified.
